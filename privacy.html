Privacy Policy

Applies to the KidsGuard browser extension. Last updated: November 17, 2026.

In short: KidsGuard has no accounts and no servers of its own. Your settings and your child's usage stay in Chrome on your computer. We do not collect, sell or share browsing history. The only thing that ever leaves your computer is the parent email address you choose to enter, and only to send you an alert.

What KidsGuard stores, and where

DataWhere it is kept
Your settings: blocked or allowed sites, schedules, time limits, blocked-page message, on/off switches Chrome's extension storage (chrome.storage.sync). If you are signed in to Chrome with sync on, Google syncs it between your own devices.
Admin password and recovery code Same place, as a one-way salted hash. The password itself is never stored.
Parent email address (optional) Same place. See "Uninstall alerts" below for when it is sent.
Today's time spent per website (for time limits and the "Today" view) and the count of blocked visits Only on this computer (chrome.storage.local). It is never uploaded.

You can remove all of it at any time by uninstalling the extension.

What leaves your computer

Only the parent email address, and only in these two cases:

  • Test email. When you save a parent email in settings, KidsGuard opens a page that sends a test message to that address, so you can confirm alerts will arrive.
  • Uninstall alert. If the extension is removed, Chrome opens an alert page that emails you about it.

In both cases the page is hosted on GitHub Pages (mcsrichard.github.io), and the email is delivered through FormSubmit. Both services receive your email address to do this, and GitHub may record the page request in its server logs. The message contains only the alert text and the time — no browsing data. If you do not enter an email, nothing is sent.

What we never do

  • No analytics, tracking pixels, advertising or third-party scripts in the extension.
  • No browsing history, page contents, keystrokes or screenshots are collected or sent anywhere.
  • No data is sold, rented or shared for marketing.

Why KidsGuard asks for its permissions

  • Read and change data on all websites (<all_urls>): to block any site you add, apply SafeSearch on search engines, and hide YouTube Shorts on youtube.com. Blocking decisions are made inside Chrome; page contents are not collected or sent anywhere.
  • Tabs and web navigation: to know which site is open, so time limits can be counted and applied, and so a site that runs out of time is blocked straight away.
  • declarativeNetRequest: Chrome's built-in way to block sites.
  • Idle: to stop counting screen time when nobody is using the computer.
  • Storage, alarms, notifications: to save settings, check limits every minute, and warn before time runs out.

Children's privacy

KidsGuard is set up and controlled by a parent or guardian. It does not ask children for any personal information and sends nothing about them off the computer.

Changes to this policy

If a future version starts sending any new data (for example an optional weekly email report), this page will be updated first and the feature will be off until you turn it on.
